How to play

Best of 3 rounds, 99 seconds each. Hold away from your opponent to block. Light attacks are fast, heavies hurt, specials have a gimmick per fighter. One air attack per jump.

Mechanics

Lows & overheadsCrouching attacks hit low — block them crouching. Air attacks are overheads — block them standing.
ThrowLight + Heavy together. Unblockable, short range — the answer to turtling. Whiffs on airborne or stunned opponents.
Dash / backdashDouble-tap a direction (or the dash button on pad). Backdash is briefly invulnerable.
SuperHeavy + Special with a full meter (the cyan bar). Every fighter has one. Big damage, big drama.
Parasoul chainsTap Light or Heavy again after contact to chain W→M→H. Forward/back/down plus an attack selects her command normals; directions plus Special select Shot, Toss, Quake, Luger and Call.
Parasoul extrasForward + Light + Heavy: F+HP. Back + Light + Heavy: Snapback. In the air, back/down + Heavy use their unique attacks. Forward + Heavy + Special at full meter: Silent Scope; neutral: Crimson Hunting. Press all three during hitstun to Burst (half meter), or from neutral to taunt.
Achilles heelSarge counters Truck: Truck takes 30% more damage and extra stun from Sarge. Reel In and Sarge's super break armor.
MeterBuilds from dealing and taking hits. Carries across rounds.
Juggle limitMax 4 hits on an airborne opponent, then they drop free. No infinites.

Player 2 — controller

Plug in (or pair) an Xbox, PlayStation or generic controller — the browser picks it up automatically. With one controller it drives Player 2; with two controllers, each player gets one. Default buttons follow the fighting-game standard (SF6 Modern / Guilty Gear); change them in Controls on the main menu. Menus are fully controller-navigable too: d-pad or stick moves, A/Cross confirms, B/Circle goes back (keyboard: arrows + Enter).

Move / crouchD-pad or left stick
Light attackX / Square
Heavy attackB / Circle
SpecialY / Triangle or RB
JumpA / Cross or up

Touch (phone / tablet)

On touch devices the game shows its own controls: the left half of the screen is a floating stick — touch anywhere and drag — and the attack buttons sit on the right. THROW and SUPER press their two-button combos for you; press both at once to Burst. Turn the device sideways.

No controller at all? Two can still share the keyboard the old cramped way: P1 on WASD+FGH, P2 on arrows+JKL. Not recommended for friendships.

Online

Keyboard (either layout) or controller, whatever feels right. Esc opens the forfeit prompt. The match runs peer-to-peer — the number in the corner is your connection delay.

Tiers

Every fighter gets a power score computed from its stats and frame data — that's the tier badge on the select screen. Community fighters are rated by the exact same formula.

Playing solo

ArcadeFive fights up a ladder, rising skill, a buffed boss at the top.
Solo vs CPUOne match against the computer at the skill you pick. The CPU reads each fighter's own data, so it plays community characters too.
TrainingEndless practice: dummy behaviour, visible hitboxes, live frame data and frame advantage, infinite health/meter. T hides the panel, R resets positions. The place to test a fighter you just made.

Community fighters

The select screen has the whole community roster under the official five — search it, sort it, play anything. Make your own with the Fighter Creator (no code needed) or hand-write one with the modding guide. Publishing needs a Chill Zone account; playing doesn't.

Community fighters have no balance limits — expect chip damage through your block, lifesteal, chain combos, invincible reversals, arcing / bouncing / piercing projectiles and their own Burst (all three attack buttons during hitstun, costs half meter). The tier badge tells you what you're walking into.

Controller mapping

Click an action, then press the button you want on your controller. Movement is always the d-pad or left stick, and up always jumps. Saved on this browser.

Keyboard layouts are fixed for now — see How to Play.
